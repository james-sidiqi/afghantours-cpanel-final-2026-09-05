## Kabul
URL: `/destinations/kabul/`

H1: Kabul
Subhead: The capital at the crossroads — layered, loud, and still the way most journeys begin.

Overview:
Kabul is not a museum. It is the city where Afghanistan works, argues, prays, and sells tea before sunrise. Older quarters sit against later palaces, Soviet monuments, and hillsides of houses. Walk Chicken Street if you want the antique trade. Climb toward the Sakhi Shrine when you want the city in one view. Spend an hour in Babur’s Gardens when you want quiet. The city rewards people who are patient with traffic and interested in ordinary life.

Logistics:
- Best time: April–May and September–November
- Arrive: Kabul International Airport (KBL)
- Pace: Light to moderate walking

## Bamyan
URL: `/destinations/bamyan/`

H1: Bamyan
Subhead: Empty Buddha niches, highland fields, and the lakes at Band-e-Amir.

Overview:
The valley sits high and open. The giant Buddhas are gone — destroyed in 2001 — and the niches are still the first thing you see. Hazara farming villages work the floor of the valley. Band-e-Amir is a separate day when the road and the weather allow it: mineral-blue lakes under the Hindu Kush. Bamyan is slower than Kabul. Plan for altitude and for days that start early so you are not crossing passes in the dark.

Logistics:
- Best time: May–June and September–October
- Getting there: Overland from Kabul by 4WD, usually 8–10 hours. The road is chosen for conditions and schedule — Hajigak / Wardak or another pass, not a fixed name on the page.
- Pace: Moderate; altitude
- Permits: Provincial tourist permits are processed in advance for booked tours.

## Herat
URL: `/destinations/herat/`

H1: Herat
Subhead: The Pearl of Khorasan — Timurid tilework, covered bazaars, and a slower western city.

Overview:
Herat shows itself in pieces: minarets on the plain, then the old city’s lanes, then the Friday Mosque’s tilework. Carpet workshops and saffron sellers still work the bazaars. The citadel stands on ground long associated with Alexander; the walls you walk are later. Sunsets here are the color of the city’s brick. Herat is quieter than Kabul. Give it time.

Logistics:
- Best time: April–May and September–October
- Getting there: Domestic flight when available, or a long overland route planned around conditions
- Pace: Light to moderate
- Permits: Processed in advance for booked tours

## Panjshir
URL: `/destinations/panjshir/`

H1: Panjshir
Subhead: A narrow valley north of Kabul — river, terraces, and a long local memory.

Overview:
From Kabul the road follows the gorge until the cliffs close in and the Panjshir River runs beside you. Villages offer tea quickly. The valley has a modern political history that travelers have heard of; we do not sell that history as a security rating, and we do not call the valley safe. What we can plan is a day or two of landscape, conversation, and, when it makes sense, the emerald-working towns. Access is assessed before each departure.

Logistics:
- Best time: May–June and September–October
- Getting there: Several hours by 4WD from Kabul when the road is open to the itinerary
- Pace: Light to moderate
- Planning: Conditions first. No fixed “always included on the weekend tour.”

# 3. CENTRAL AFGHANISTAN DISCOVERY
URL: `/tours/central-afghanistan-discovery/`

Keep the live 7-day spine. Rewrite the sentences. Do not invent a new route.

H1: Central Afghanistan Discovery
Subhead: 7 days · Kabul to Bamyan and back

At a glance:
- Duration: 7 days / 6 nights
- Style: Cities, highland road, Bamyan valley, Band-e-Amir when conditions allow
- Pace: Moderate
- Group: Private / small group

Overview:
Seven days from Kabul into Bamyan and back. The road day is long. The valley days are the reason for the road. Band-e-Amir is a full day when the weather and the road agree. Final hotels, timing, and price are confirmed after we know group size, dates, and current access.

Letter of Invitation support is included for every booked tour. We do not issue visas. A written proposal lists transport, hotels, permits, and what is not included.

## Day 1 — Arrive Kabul
Airport reception as specified on your proposal. A light first day: an older quarter such as Murad Khane, time in Babur’s Gardens if the light and the traffic allow it, and an evening briefing.

## Day 2 — Kabul to Bamyan
Early start. The highland road is chosen that week — Hajigak / Wardak or another pass — based on conditions and schedule. Lunch is wherever the day has put you. You want to reach Bamyan with daylight on the niches.

## Day 3 — Bamyan valley
The Buddha niches, Shahr-e-Gholghola, and Shahr-e-Zohak. Walk more than you photograph at first. The empty alcoves are the fact of the valley; the fortress ruins are the rest of the day.

## Day 4 — Band-e-Amir
A full day to the lakes when the road and the weather are suitable. If they are not, this becomes more time in the Bamyan valley. We do not force the lakes in bad conditions.

## Day 5 — Return to Kabul
Back through the central highlands. The day is also a buffer for weather and punctures. Evening in Kabul.

## Day 6 — Kabul, slower
National Museum, Darulaman, Sakhi Shrine, or a viewpoint — chosen for energy and for what was closed yesterday. Chicken Street if you still have the legs.

## Day 7 — Depart
Transfer as specified on the proposal.
