### Overview
Herat is the architectural and intellectual jewel of Western Afghanistan. Known as the "Pearl of Khorasan," it sits in the fertile Hari River valley and boasts a history that spans millennia, notably serving as a center of the Timurid Renaissance. The city is defined by its sophisticated Persian-influenced culture, ancient mud-brick old city, and towering minarets.

For travelers, Herat offers a refined atmosphere distinct from the rest of the country. It is a city of poets, artisans, and historical depth, where the call to prayer echoes off 800-year-old mosaic tiles. Historically and operationally, it serves as the primary gateway for western trade and cultural exchange.

### Why Travelers Stay Here

Travelers stay in Herat to witness some of the finest Islamic architecture in the world. The city features a high concentration of heritage sites within walking distance of the center. It offers unique markets specializing in Herati glass and silk, a wide range of comfortable accommodations, and a distinct regional cuisine.

### Major Nearby Attractions

* The Great Mosque of Herat (Masjid-i Jami)
* The Citadel of Herat (Qala Ikhtyaruddin)
* Musalla Complex and Minarets
* Tomb of Gohar Shad
* Herat Old Bazaar and Caravanserai

### Travel Role

Herat functions as the Western architectural hub and a primary logistics center for the border region. It serves as:
* An entry point for overland travelers from the west.
* A domestic flight hub connecting Western Afghanistan to Kabul.
* A resupply point for expeditions into the central Ghor province.

### Visitor Notes

The "Wind of 120 Days" can bring high winds and dust during the summer months. Traffic in the old city is dense and best navigated by foot or rickshaw. Visitors should plan for several days here to appreciate the hand-tiled workshops at the Great Mosque. Photography at the Citadel is generally permitted but requires a ticket.
