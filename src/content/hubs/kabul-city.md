### Overview
Kabul is the high-altitude capital and the primary entry point for nearly every international traveler visiting the country. Set in a narrow valley surrounded by the Hindu Kush mountains, the city is a dense, high-energy metropolis where ancient mud-brick neighborhoods cling to hillsides overlooking modern diplomatic districts and bustling commercial centers.

Historically, Kabul has served as a strategic prize for empires due to its position on the Silk Road, linking Central and South Asia. Today, it remains the administrative and logistical heart of the nation. For travelers, the city offers a unique atmosphere that blends traditional bazaars and street food culture with the logistical infrastructure required for modern expeditions.

### Why Travelers Stay Here

Kabul serves as the central node for all domestic travel. It hosts the country’s primary international airport (KBL) and is the staging ground for almost all overland tours. Travelers stay here to enjoy the nation's highest concentration of quality hotels, diverse restaurants ranging from traditional kabob houses to international fare, and expansive markets like the famous Bird Market (Ka Faroshi). It is also the most reliable location for resupplying technical gear or specialized provisions.

### Travel Role

Operationally, Kabul is the ultimate logistics center. It functions as the primary overnight stop upon arrival, the central base for mountain expeditions, and the main hub for domestic flight connections. Most importantly, it is the center for permit coordination and administrative clearance before heading into the provinces.

### Visitor Notes

Kabul sits at an altitude of approximately 1,790 meters, so travelers should stay hydrated to adjust. Traffic is notoriously heavy; movement timing is critical, especially when heading to the airport. While the city offers the best hotel standards in the country, electricity and internet can be intermittent. Photography of sensitive government buildings is strictly prohibited, though street photography is generally welcomed with permission.
