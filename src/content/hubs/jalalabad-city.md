### Overview
Jalalabad is a sun-drenched city in the eastern lowlands, known for its citrus groves, public gardens, and winter warmth. Located at the junction of the Kabul and Kunar rivers, it has historically served as a winter capital for Afghan rulers and a vital link between Kabul and the Khyber Pass.

The geography is dominated by fertile plains surrounded by rugged mountains. It is a bustling commercial center with a distinct lowland energy, characterized by its fruit markets and palm-lined avenues.

### Why Travelers Stay Here

Travelers stay here to escape the winter cold of Kabul and to explore the unique Buddhist archaeological sites in the surrounding valley. It is a hub for the best street food in the east, particularly fish from the Darunta Dam, and offers a lively market scene selling fresh produce and spices.

### Major Nearby Attractions

* Mausoleum of King Amanullah Khan
* Siraj-ul-Emarat Park
* Hadda Archaeological Site
* Darunta Dam and Reservoir

### Travel Role

Jalalabad is the Eastern lowland gateway. It functions as:
* The primary transit point for travelers heading toward the Torkham border.
* A winter retreat and lowland logistics center.
* A gateway to the mountainous Kunar and Nuristan regions.

### Visitor Notes

Traffic can be chaotic, particularly near the central bazaar. Unlike the rest of the country, Jalalabad remains lush and green throughout the winter. Photography is generally relaxed in the public gardens, but as a major transit hub, travelers should be mindful of security checkpoints around the city perimeter.
