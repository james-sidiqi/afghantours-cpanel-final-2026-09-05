To travel during a major holiday is to see Afghanistan at its most social and reflective. New clothing, family visits, prayers, sweets, tea, and open doors define the rhythm of celebration.

## Eid Traditions

Eid al-Fitr marks the end of Ramadan, while Eid al-Adha centers on sacrifice, sharing, and family gatherings. In both, children receive **Eidi**, guests move between homes, and tables fill with dried fruits, noql, nuts, pastries, and tea.

## Hospitality at Home

The most important holiday scenes often happen inside homes. Guests are welcomed, seated, fed, and encouraged to stay. Even a short visit becomes part of a wider social network.

## Cultural Pairing: Kocha and Festive Sweets

During Eid al-Adha, hearty dishes such as **Kocha** may appear alongside sweets, tea, dried fruits, and fresh bread. The food reflects the obligation to share blessings with family, neighbors, and those in need.
