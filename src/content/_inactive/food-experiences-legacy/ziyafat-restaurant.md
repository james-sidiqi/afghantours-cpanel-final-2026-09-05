The word **ziyafat** means feast or banquet, and the idea sits at the center of Afghan hospitality. A meal is not only food; it is a way to honor guests.

## A Table Built for Sharing

Afghan dining is often communal. Large platters arrive with naan, salads, chutneys, yogurt sauces, rice, dumplings, grilled meats, and tea. Guests are encouraged to eat more, stay longer, and enjoy the company.

## The Feast Experience

A strong Afghan feast may include Qabuli Palaw, mantu, aushak, kabobs, bolani, fresh naan, seasonal fruits, and sweets. The abundance itself is part of the message.

## Cultural Pairing: Afghan Hospitality

The true pairing is hospitality. The conversation, the insistence that guests continue eating, and the flow of tea after the meal are what travelers remember most.
