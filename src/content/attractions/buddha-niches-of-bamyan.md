### Short Introduction
Iconic Silk Road cultural giants. Carved into the sandstone cliffs in the 6th century, these
massive niches once housed the world's tallest standing Buddhas. Today, they remain a UNESCO
World Heritage site and a powerful symbol of Afghanistan's diverse history, offering panoramic
views of the valley from the honeycomb of caves and monastic cells.

### Cultural / Historical Context
This is a history-focused stop where the surrounding setting is part of the experience. Ruins,
surviving structures, and local place memory help visitors understand how older political,
religious, and trade networks shaped this part of Afghanistan.

### Visitor Reality
Plan this as a 3-hour visit. Access is generally straightforward by Afghan travel standards,
but timing still depends on local coordination, road conditions, and the day’s operating plan.
The preferred movement style is road. Morning and late afternoon usually give the best light
for photography and a more comfortable pace for walking.
