To understand Afghanistan, one must look at its carpets. Across northern plains, western bazaars, and mountain villages, carpets are more than trade goods. They are family history, regional identity, and portable art.

## A Language Written in Knots

Afghan carpets often communicate through geometry. The **gul** motif, protective diamonds, tree-of-life patterns, borders, and repeated symbols can identify tribal groups, regions, and family traditions.

Many carpets use colors drawn from the landscape: madder reds, indigo blues, pomegranate yellows, walnut browns, and naturally shifting tones known as **abrash**.

## Regional Styles

Northern Turkmen carpets are known for deep reds and strong gul designs. Uzbek and Turkmen-influenced styles around Andkhoy and Mazar may use brighter geometric forms. Baluch kilims from western and southwestern communities are lighter, flatter, and practical for nomadic life.

## The Visitor Experience

A carpet stop can be part shopping, part cultural lesson. Good guides help visitors understand wool quality, knot density, dyes, regional patterns, and how to browse respectfully without pressure.

## Cultural Pairing: Green Tea on the Charpai

Carpets are not museum pieces. In Afghan homes and tea houses, life happens on the floor or on raised platforms covered with layered rugs. Green tea, almonds, raisins, and conversation complete the experience.
